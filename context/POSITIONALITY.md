# Positionality Statement

## Who We Are

**Zolai-AI** is a community-driven initiative focused on preserving and revitalizing the Tedim Zolai language through AI technology.

### Our Relationship to the Language

- **peterlianpi (Project Lead):** Native Zomi speaker, technologist, language advocate
- **Community contributors:** Native speakers, linguists, educators, developers
- **Institutional affiliation:** Independent, community-owned

### Why This Matters

In low-resource language NLP, positionality affects:
1. **Data collection** — Who we approach, how we ask, what we collect
2. **Annotation** — What counts as "correct," whose variant is standard
3. **Evaluation** — What metrics matter, who defines success
4. **Deployment** — Who benefits, who is excluded

## Our Position

### As Native Speakers
- We understand the language's nuances, variants, and cultural context
- We can distinguish authentic from generated text
- We know which data sources are trustworthy
- We can validate cultural accuracy

### As Technologists
- We have skills to build AI systems
- We understand data pipelines, models, and deployment
- We can bridge community needs with technical possibilities
- We must avoid "tech solutionism" — not everything needs AI

### As Community Members
- We are accountable to the Zomi community
- We must prioritize community benefit over publication
- We must respect cultural protocols and sensitivities
- We must ensure data sovereignty

## Acknowledged Limitations

### What We Don't Know
- Expert-level linguistics (grammar formalism, phonology)
- All dialectal variants (we focus on Tedim)
- Sacred/ceremonial language use
- Historical language evolution

### What We Can't Do Alone
- Validate without community input
- Represent all Zomi perspectives
- Ensure no harm without oversight
- Build sustainable systems without funding

### What We Choose Not to Do
- Extract data without consent
- Publish without community review
- Deploy without accessibility testing
- Claim ownership of community knowledge

## Commitments

### To the Community
1. **Transparency** — Open-source code, public documentation
2. **Accountability** — Regular reporting, community oversight
3. **Reciprocity** — Benefits flow back to community
4. **Respect** — Cultural protocols honored

### To Other Researchers
1. **Honesty** — Acknowledge limitations and failures
2. **Reproducibility** — Document methods and data
3. **Collaboration** — Share tools and knowledge
4. **Credit** — Acknowledge all contributions

### To the Language
1. **Accuracy** — ZVS 2018 compliance, native speaker validation
2. **Completeness** — Cover all registers and domains
3. **Accessibility** — Multiple formats, offline support
4. **Sustainability** — Community-owned, maintainable

## How to Cite This Work

When referencing Zolai-AI work, please include:

```
Zolai-AI. (2026). [Title]. Retrieved from [URL]

Note: This work was developed by native Zomi speakers and 
technologists in collaboration with the Zomi community. 
Positionality: The authors are native speakers with technical 
expertise, committed to community benefit and data sovereignty.
```
